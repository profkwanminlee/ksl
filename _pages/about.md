## Institutional Overview
{% include korean-title-underline.html %}

<div class="overview-lead-box">
  <p class="overview-lead-text">
    <strong>Korean Studies Lab (KSL)</strong> is an interdisciplinary research initiative based at Nanyang Technological University, Singapore. As one of the first initiatives of its kind in Southeast Asia, the Lab supports empirical research, academic publications, and public programs on contemporary Korea and its growing connections with Singapore and the ASEAN region.
  </p>
</div>

The Lab’s research agenda focuses on three primary areas of contemporary significance:
- **Korea’s IT innovation and digital society**
- **Contemporary Korean culture and the Korean Wave**
- **Human and cultural exchange between Korea and Southeast Asia**

Building on existing scholarship and active international partnerships, the Lab examines how Korea is interpreted, translated, and engaged across diverse cultural, social, and technological contexts. Focusing particularly on contemporary media infrastructures, the Lab investigates the mechanisms through which digital platforms, algorithmic systems, and creative industries shape cultural transformation across Asia.

Through symposia, research colloquia, visiting fellowships, and joint publications, the Lab brings together scholars, students, and institutional partners across Singapore, South Korea, and Southeast Asia.

## Three Core Research Focus Areas
{% include korean-title-underline.html %}

The Lab organizes its scholarly programs around three interconnected domains:

### 1. Korea’s IT Innovation and Digital Society
South Korea stands as a global frontier for digital infrastructure, platform innovation, and technological adaptation. This research area examines how digital infrastructures, algorithmic systems, and emerging technologies (including artificial intelligence, smart interfaces, and virtual environments) transform daily social life, labor, communication, and governance. Special attention is directed toward comparative analyses of digital transformation between South Korea and Southeast Asian technology centers such as Singapore.

### 2. Contemporary Korean Culture and the Korean Wave
The transnational circulation of contemporary Korean culture—popularly known as the *Korean Wave* or *Hallyu*—has reshaped regional and global cultural landscapes. This area investigates how Korean screen media, music, digital entertainment, and creative industries circulate across Singapore and Southeast Asian societies. Rather than treating this phenomenon solely as cultural export, the Lab investigates the complex processes of reception, cultural translation, local appropriation, and identity construction.

### 3. Human and Cultural Exchange between Korea and Southeast Asia
Historically and in the contemporary era, the movement of people, ideas, and institutional frameworks between Korea and Southeast Asia forms a dynamic zone of interaction. This pillar focuses on educational mobility, migration, diplomatic and policy dialogues, diaspora communities, and multilateral institutional partnerships connecting Korea with the ASEAN region.
