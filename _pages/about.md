## Overview

**Korean Studies Lab** is an interdisciplinary research initiative based at Nanyang Technological University, Singapore. As one of the first initiatives of its kind in Southeast Asia, the Lab supports research, publications, and public programs on contemporary Korea.

The Lab’s current research focuses on three main areas:
- **Korea’s IT innovation and digital society**
- **Contemporary Korean culture and the Korean Wave**
- **Human and cultural exchange between Korea and Southeast Asia**

Building on existing research and ongoing collaborations, the Lab examines how Korea is interpreted, translated, and discussed in different cultural, social, and political contexts.

Focusing on contemporary Korean culture and its circulation across Singapore and Southeast Asia, the Lab pays particular attention to the ways digital infrastructures, digital media, and technological systems shape cultural exchange and transformation across the region.

The Lab brings together scholars, students, and institutional partners through research, publications, and public programs. It aims to foster dialogue and build collaborations with academic, cultural, and policy institutions across Asia.

---

## Mission

The Contemporary Korea Studies Lab is guided by three core mandates:

1. **Advancing the study of contemporary Korea in Singapore and Southeast Asia.**  
   Fostering empirical and critical scholarship that addresses Korea’s social, technological, and cultural dynamics from a Southeast Asian vantage point.

2. **Supporting research, dialogue, and exchange on Korea in Southeast Asia.**  
   Providing institutional mechanisms, funding pathways, and collaborative platforms for faculty, researchers, and students across regional institutions.

3. **Serving as a hub for research and engagement on contemporary Korea in Southeast Asia.**  
   Connecting academic communities with cultural institutions, policy makers, industry leaders, and the broader public to deepen regional understanding.

## Research Focus

The Lab structures its scholarly initiatives around three interrelated focal areas:

### 1. Korea’s IT Innovation and Digital Society
South Korea stands as a global frontier for digital infrastructure, platform design, and technological adaptation. This research area examines how digital infrastructures, algorithmic systems, and emerging technologies (such as artificial intelligence, virtual environments, and smart media platforms) transform daily social life, labor, communication, and governance. Special attention is directed toward comparative analyses of digital transformation across Korea and Southeast Asian tech hubs.

### 2. Contemporary Korean Culture and the Korean Wave
The transnational diffusion of contemporary Korean culture—popularly known as the *Korean Wave* or *Hallyu*—has reshaped regional and global cultural landscapes. This area investigates how Korean screen media, music, digital entertainment, and creative industries circulate across Singapore and Southeast Asian societies. Rather than viewing this phenomenon solely as cultural export, the Lab investigates the complex processes of reception, cultural translation, local appropriation, and identity construction.

### 3. Human and Cultural Exchange between Korea and Southeast Asia
Historically and in the contemporary era, the movement of people, ideas, and institutional frameworks between Korea and Southeast Asia forms a dynamic zone of interaction. This pillar focuses on educational mobility, migration, diplomatic and policy dialogues, diaspora communities, and multilateral institutional partnerships connecting Korea with the ASEAN region.

## Institutional Context

The Contemporary Korea Studies Lab is hosted at the **Wee Kim Wee School of Communication and Information (WKWSCI)** at **Nanyang Technological University, Singapore (NTU Singapore)**.

Established in connection with the **Korea Foundation Endowed Chair in Contemporary Korean Society and New Media**, the Lab operates as an open, collaborative academic space linking researchers across NTU’s College of Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences (CoHASS), the College of Computing and Data Science, and international partner universities across Korea, Southeast Asia, and North America.
